source: [The Insider's Guide to Data Rooms: What to Know Before You Raise | Andreessen Horowitz](https://a16z.com/2022/08/25/the-insiders-guide-to-data-rooms-what-to-know-before-you-raise/)

- pitch deck
    - 会社の理念
    - 製品ビジョン
    - 競合状況
    - トラクション
    - チーム
    - 資金をどのように使うのかのロードマップ
- 資本表
    - 株主の投資額
    - 株主構成
- 過去の損益とバーンレート
    - 売上
    - 純利益
    - 現金流出のキャッシュフロー
- プロダクトの利用に関するデータ
    - 利用者成長（ユーザー数、登録者数）
    - 獲得チャンネル（どのチャンネルからアクセスしているのか）
    - エンゲージメント（セッション時間）
    - リテンション（どの程度定着しているのか）
- LTV（顧客生涯価値）/CAC（顧客獲得コスト）と投資回収期間
    - LTV/CAC >1になっているか（顧客単位で利益を生み出せているか）
- 逆に細かすぎる情報はいらない
    - チームの経歴・組織図
    - 3年~5年の財務諸表
    - 法的書類
    - マーケットサイジング（未知数なマーケットは入れた方がいい場合もある）

> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204163501203458): /1 Here's the "pencil and notepad" slide deck process that got my company $8.5M in a bear market.
> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204166026125312): /2 START WITH PEN AND PAPER to craft a V1 story without getting side-tracked.
> 1 — Write headlines on paper
> 2 — Make headlines flow like a paragraph
> 3 — Edit, cut, and repeat until the story is obvious
> I mostly use Things 3 from @culturedcode to write with no distractions.
> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204168668532736?s=20): /3 ONLY WORK ON HEADLINES until they are *instantly* understandable. Great headlines spoon-feed quantitative information.
> "Market Size" is bad. "The fragmented $15B lemonade market in India is growing 75% YoY" is great.
> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204168668532736?s=20): /3 ONLY WORK ON HEADLINES until they are *instantly* understandable. Great headlines spoon-feed quantitative information.
> "Market Size" is bad. "The fragmented $15B lemonade market in India is growing 75% YoY" is great.
> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204177048842241?s=20): /4 ADOPT THE FORMAT shared by @sequoia. Not because VCs like it — it's just a great structure to think about your business.
> Stick to a max of 15 slides per deck + an appendix.
> Put everything beyond these topics in the appendix.
> ![image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/Fe4DUo2VsAAuRk4.jpg)
> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204180068659202?s=20): /5 TEACH 1 THING ON EVERY SLIDE — Investors look for frontier learnings. Every slide should teach 1 insight.
> Test insight quality. Ask yourself "will investors walk away feeling like they now know something others don't?"
> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204182794997761?s=20): /6 PRIORITIZE NON-OBVIOUS PROBLEMS.
> "NFTs are a $50B market" is obvious. "Companies like X and Y are replacing Adwords with NFTs to reduce CAC by 80%" is less obvious + more precise.
> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204185403805696?s=20): /7 VISUALIZE YOUR AUDIENCE of VCs in SF who see dozens of pitches per week. You should assume they'll spend 90-120 seconds on your deck.
> Put yourself in their shoes. Then review your headlines again.
> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204187970785280?s=20): /8 GRAPHICS ARE 20% OF THE WORK — only once you've *nailed* headlines, move to graphics. Graphics only exist to support what headlines say.
> For graphics, not all data is equal: Causal > Quantitative Correlated > Qualitative > Annectdotal.
> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204190625763328?s=20): /9 SLIDES ARE FOR THE PROBLEM, not the solution.
> Most great decks are 90% market and problem, and only 10% solution. By the end of the deck, the solution (your project) should be obvious.
> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204193372962821?s=20): /10 POLISH IN FIGMA OR KEYNOTE — PowerPoint and Google Slides don't cut it. Many people feel the difference right away.
> For pre-seeds, a simple @NotionHQ page can work if your headlines are really great.
> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204199874134016?s=20): /11 TRACK DECK METRICS — you can learn in real time how well your deck is doing by tracking views and time spent on key slides.
> I *always* use @DocSend to learn if my audience cares most about market size, revenue model, other investors, etc.
> ![image](https://pbs.twimg.com/media/Fe4DV9EUAAAw8Ar.png)
> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204202915074048?s=20): /12 CUT, EDIT, AND ADD early on and throughout the process.
> Pitching is like teaching a course. If your students don't get it, you need to adapt to keep them interested.
> [@laliberteg](https://twitter.com/laliberteg/status/1580204205565878273?s=20): /13 The pitch deck mindset:
> You have about 60s to teach something. Your students have a 6s attention span and see 20 pitches per day. They'll only remember 1) unique insights, 2) the problem, and 3) whether they felt FOMO or not.
